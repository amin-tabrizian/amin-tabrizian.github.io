I'm a PhD candidate in Computer Science at George Washington University, where I work with Peng Wei in the Intelligent Aerospace Systems Lab. Most of my research is about LLM agents and reinforcement learning in aviation and driving, and about how to tell whether they are safe enough to use there. My flight planning work is funded by NASA and done together with MIT Lincoln Laboratory and Honeywell.

This fall I'm teaching Large Language and Vision Models (MAE 6291 / CSCI 6907), a graduate course at GW. Since June I've also been an AI engineer intern at Cignus Consulting, where I build and red-team an LLM agent for aviation intelligence.

Before GW, I studied mechanical engineering at the University of Tehran.

IoT-J

Delay-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Highway On-Ramp Merging under Stochastic Communication Latency

A. Tabrizian, Z. Huang, A. Aziz, P. Wei

IEEE Internet of Things Journal, 2026

A roadside unit tells the merging car where the other cars are, but that picture can be up to two seconds old. We treat merging as a random-delay MDP and give a SAC agent a GRU encoder that recovers the current state from stale observations and its own recent actions, plus a physics-based safety check. Over 99% merge success in dense SUMO traffic built from NGSIM data.

ICML-W

End-to-End LLM Flight Planning with RAG-based Memory and Multi-modal Coach Agent

A. Tabrizian, A. Aziz, A. Ullah, M. Ghazanfari, P. Razzaghi, P. Wei

ICML 2026 Workshop on Planning in the Era of Language Models (LM4Plan)

An LLM writes eVTOL flight plans as lists of waypoints. A second, multimodal agent looks at a rendered map of each plan and critiques it, and a retrieval memory brings back earlier plans and their reviews. Across four planner models, the full system gives the most valid plans, up to 93.8%.

TR-E

Adaptive Air Traffic Management via Large Language Model Augmented Reinforcement Learning

S. Chen, A. Tabrizian, P. Wei, Y. Wang, X. Qu

Transportation Research Part E: Logistics and Transportation Review, vol. 214, 2026

For urban air mobility traffic, an LLM proposes reward-weight settings offline, and the system later picks among pre-trained multi-agent RL policies to match what the user cares about. This takes most of the manual reward tuning out of the loop.

paper

A Paragraph is Worth a Thousand Captions: Rethinking Text Supervision for Vision-Language Retrieval

M. Ghazanfari, A. Tabrizian, A. Aziz, B. Wang, P. Wei

arXiv preprint, 2026

CLIP-style retrievers learn from short captions and struggle with long descriptions. Fine-tuning only BLIP's text encoder on synthetic paragraphs for 500K images matches Long-CLIP-L on ShareGPT4V and beats it by more than 14 points on DOCCI, with no change to the architecture.

arXiv

ICML-W

EO-Agents: A Three-Agent LLM Pipeline for Earth Observation Hypothesis Generation

M. Ghazanfari, A. Tabrizian, A. Mehrabian, P. Wei

ICML 2026 Workshop on AI for Science

A graph neural network over NASA's Earth observation knowledge graph suggests which datasets to study together, and three LLM agents filter the pairs, write hypotheses, and grade them. From 1,475 datasets we get 160 hypotheses. The ranking holds up across different judge models even though the raw scores do not.

arXivworkshop

AIAA

Towards Automated Air Traffic Safety Assessment Around Non-Towered Airports Using Large Language Models

T. Darrell, M. Ghazanfari, J. K. Kam, A. Bayen, A. Tabrizian, P. Wei

AIAA Aviation Forum, 2026

At airports without a tower, pilots keep each other safe by announcing themselves on a shared radio frequency. We give radio transcripts, weather reports, ADS-B tracks, and VFR charts to vision-language models for post-flight safety review. On a nominal vs. danger task, even open models reach a macro F1 above 0.85.

paperarXiv

AIAA

Chain-of-Thought Flight Planner: End-to-End LLM Routing Under Wind Hazards

A. Tabrizian, M. Ghazanfari, P. Wei

AIAA Aviation Forum, Las Vegas, 2025

Best Student Paper Award, Air Transportation Systems

We ask an LLM to route an air taxi around wind hazard zones while following what the operator says they want, and compare five prompting strategies. The best one gives a valid plan 98% of the time.
